I've watched the same painful mistake burn through millions of pounds. I built a system to prevent it.

    Someone briefs a team, assuming everyone understands. The team nods along, not wanting to admit confusion. Everyone walks away with a different vision in their head, but they start building anyway.

    I've used this with scrappy two-person startups and global enterprise teams. The size changes, but the problem doesn't. Everyone assumes their ideas are obvious. Everyone benefits from clarity before execution.

  • The result

    Savings, both time and money. Clarity first means you build the right product, faster, and cheaper. Your team is executing a shared vision instead of expensive guesswork.

The Missing Link

About Bret: The Human Behind the Method

Who I Am, How I Work, and Whether We're a Good Fit.

Early in my career, I noticed something that changed everything: people assumed their ideas were obvious.

The pattern that shaped my career

I kept watching the same thing: someone would brief a team orally "Build me a reporting dashboard" or "We need a better onboarding flow" and walk away thinking they'd been perfectly clear. Meanwhile, the team sat there confused but nodding, each person forming their own interpretation.

Nobody asked questions. Nobody wanted to look like they didn't get it. So everyone just started building based on their best guess.

Six months and hundreds of thousands of pounds later, everyone would realise: we weren't building the same product at all.

I became obsessed with solving this

Not through better communication tools or endless meetings, but through a methodology that evolves and creates clarity before execution. One that ensures everyone sees the same picture: what we're building, why it matters, how it works in the real world, and whether the technical team can actually deliver it.

When you get that clarity first, everything changes. You build the best product, quickly, at the lowest cost, because you're not guessing, you are executing a shared vision.

Why I do this work

Honestly? I love the chaos and possibility of early-stage thinking. Give me a napkin sketch and a passionate individual, and I'm happier than most people are on vacation. There's something deeply satisfying about taking the abstract and making it concrete.

I'm not the guy who just takes your money and delivers a document. I'm the guy who genuinely cares whether your product succeeds. I'll challenge you when your idea needs evolution, celebrate with you when we nail it, and lose sleep thinking about how to solve your toughest problems.

Those moments when everyone finally see the same picture? That's when I know we're about to build something that matters.

What working with me actually looks like

I'm direct but not harsh. I ask a lot of questions, the kind that uncover assumptions you didn't know you were making. I think out loud and sketch constantly (whiteboards are my happy place). I'll use metaphors, some that land perfectly and some that don't, and yes, I'll probably reference evolution more than is strictly necessary.

I don't do corporate speak or jargon for jargon's sake. If something doesn't make sense, I'll tell you. If your idea needs work, I'll tell you that too, but I'll help you fix it.
